Reading the WFS night reports

← calendar · basicsthresholdssummarytimelineM1 surfaceslew responsevisitseventsdata files

Each night report is built from the night's WFS frames reanalyzed offline with the current mmtwfs (reanalyze), and from the log that the online WFS server (wfssrv) wrote while observing (wfs.log). The frames give what each image measured; the log gives what was actually sent to the telescope and when. This page describes reports made with mmtwfs $version.

Basics

Nights and times
Night directories are named by UT date, so the night that starts on the evening of 2 November local time is 20261103. A directory covers local noon (MST, UTC−7) the day before its date to local noon on its date. All times in the reports are UTC.
Frame status
ok: reanalyzed successfully. failed: reanalysis couldn't measure it, usually too few WFS spots (clouds, no star, star off the WFS, or a frame taken while slewing). invalid: the wavefront fit residual is above $focus_valid nm, which wfssrv calls "wavefront fit too poor" and uses for nothing. Between $all_valid and $focus_valid nm wfssrv trusts only focus, so those frames keep their focus but their coma and delivered IQ are blanked. unanalyzed: reanalyze hasn't been run on it yet. For frames wfssrv analyzed, its own verdicts from wfs.log are used; otherwise the same residual limits are applied to the reanalysis.
Focus error
The focus correction measured from a frame, in µm of M2 hexapod motion at an M2 gain of 1, i.e. how far focus was off. Its error bar comes from the uncertainty of the fitted Z04 (defocus) term.
Coma
The M2 center-of-curvature tilt corrections (CC x, CC y, arcsec) that would remove the measured coma.
Seeing
From the width of the WFS spots, using a method derived from the VLT's, scaled to 500 nm. Raw seeing is at the airmass observed; zenith seeing is raw × airmass−0.6. A raw seeing above $max_raw_seeing″ is not physical (a bad spot fit) and is dropped, along with the zenith seeing and delivered IQ derived from it.
Delivered IQ
The FWHM (arcsec, 500 nm) of the image the telescope was delivering when the frame was taken: the PSF of the measured wavefront (tilt removed), including the MMT's pupil, convolved with the raw seeing. It differs from the raw seeing by what the optics added at that moment, so it is largest right after a slew and should drop toward the seeing as corrections are applied.
Visit
A run of consecutive frames on the same target: the same catalog ID (CATID), or, when frames have none, target positions within $slew_sep″ of each other. RA/Dec offsets within a visit, such as dithers, don't start a new visit; they're listed as offset events.

Settle thresholds

These are the defaults; the line at the top of each report states the values that report used.

quantityvaluemeaning
focus threshold$focus_thresh µmfocus counts as settled once |focus error| < this
coma threshold$coma_thresh″coma counts as settled once both |CC x| and |CC y| < this
consecutive frames$consecutivea threshold must hold for this many ok frames in a row; the settle time is that of the first frame of the run
IQ plateau tolerancelarger of $iq_abs″ or $iq_frac_pct% of the plateaufor iq_settle_conv, below
visit separation$slew_sep″frames without a catalog ID whose targets are farther apart start a new visit
M1 focus gate$m1_gate µmwfssrv sends no M1 forces while the pending focus (M2 gain × focus error) is above this; such frames are flagged m1_blocked_focus

Settle times are seconds from the visit's first frame, failed ones included, so they include the time to get the first good frame. There are two kinds:

threshold (focus_settle_thresh, coma_settle_thresh)
When the error first stays below the fixed threshold. Blank ("not settled") if it never does, e.g. in bad seeing, where frame-to-frame scatter alone exceeds the threshold.
convergence (focus_settle_conv, iq_settle_conv)
When the error first gets within reach of where the visit ended up. The plateau is the median over the second half of the visit's ok frames; focus has converged once |focus error| ≤ plateau + $focus_thresh µm, and delivered IQ once it is within the IQ tolerance above its plateau. These still give a time on nights when the threshold is never met.

F/9 visits are judged differently (settle_def = last_send): for them use settle_last_send, the time of the last correction sent during the visit.

Night timeline

Seven panels sharing a UTC time axis. In every panel a red dashed vertical line marks an M1 clear and a purple dotted one a reset of the M2 WFS offsets.

  1. Elevation. Each frame's elevation. Shaded bands are visits, alternating in color and labeled with the target's catalog ID. Red triangles near the top are flags, numbered as in the Events table; flags close together share a label such as 4–9. The legend lists them, or summarizes them by kind when there are more than $max_flags.
  2. Focus error (µm) of each ok frame with its error bar. Orange ticks along the bottom are focus corrections sent to M2. Points beyond the plotted range are drawn as red triangles on the panel edge and counted in the legend as "off scale".
  3. Coma: CC x and CC y (arcsec) of each ok frame.
  4. Seeing and IQ: raw seeing, zenith seeing, and delivered IQ (red) of each ok frame, arcsec at 500 nm. Delivered IQ above the raw seeing is what the optics were costing.
  5. Cumulative M2 offsets: running sums of what wfssrv sent to the hexapod since the last reset. Left axis, µm: wfs_z, the focus corrections, and m1spherical_z, the focus moves that accompany M1 spherical corrections. Right axis, arcsec (black dashed gridlines): wfs_cc_x, wfs_cc_y, the coma corrections. wfs_z and the coma reset on an M2 offset reset; m1spherical_z resets on an M1 clear.
  6. Cumulative M1 forces: the running total of the force sets applied to the primary since the last M1 clear (each scaled by the fraction the cell reported applying). Left: RMS force over the actuators (N). Right: RMS of the resulting surface (nm).
  7. Cumulative M1 wavefront: Zernike terms fitted to that cumulative surface, in nm of wavefront (twice the surface), in the WFS frame. Only terms that exceed 10 nm at some point are drawn.

M1 surface

Maps of the primary's surface displacement (nm) produced by the cumulative M1 forces, drawn just before each M1 clear and at the end of the night. Dots are the actuators, labeled with their IDs, and the title gives the time. "No M1 forces applied" means none were sent, or their force files aren't in the archive.

Slew response

How the telescope arrived at each visit, one point per visit:

  • top left: focus error of the visit's first ok frame against the elevation change from the previous visit (ΔEL). A trend means focus changes predictably with elevation and could be anticipated.
  • top right: the same first-frame focus against the visit's elevation.
  • bottom left: threshold focus settle time against ΔEL. Visits that never settled are absent.
  • bottom right: the dead time between tracks, per visit: the gap since the previous visit's last frame (blue) with the focus settle time stacked on it (orange). Gaps are as seen by the WFS, so they include slewing, acquisition, and restarting the WFS. Visits that never settled are marked "not settled".
